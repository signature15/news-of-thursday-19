# NOTES

## Design notes

Field notes on the layout of `entry.png`, the 1024x1024 broadsheet that carries
all of Thursday 19 November 2026 on one page.

- **Cream paper.** The page is cream rather than white because the poster is
  drawn in the manner of printed newsprint: warm stock reads as "newspaper"
  before a single headline is parsed, and it makes the white story cards sit on
  the page as a visible second layer, so hierarchy is carried by paper color as
  well as by type size.
- **The date tab.** The deep-green `19 NOV 2026` block pinned to the top-left
  corner works like the date ear of a print front page: the contest's core
  claim - this is one day - is answered at a glance, before the nameplate is
  even read. The same green is reused for the `ALSO ON THURSDAY` and
  `DATE CHECK` tabs, so green consistently marks editorial furniture, while
  red, blue, orange and gold are reserved for region coding below it.
- **The region bar.** Directly under the masthead, seven colored cells count
  the page's coverage (ASIA 8, EUROPE 8, AFRICA 7, AMERICAS 7, US 3, OCEANIA 3,
  MIDDLE EAST 2). It doubles as a legend: every card header and every brief is
  tagged in its region's color, so the nameplate's "seven regions" promise is
  auditable in one sweep.
- **Eight cells in the numbers strip.** `THE DAY IN NUMBERS` runs eight figures
  because the day produced eight headline numbers worth remembering: the ICJ's
  9-6 genocide vote, Anthropic's $81bn IPO price and $2tn open, the M5.7
  Kumamoto quake, UK inflation at 3.6%, the EUR45bn loan to Ukraine, Harmer's
  6-51 spell, and the 56-44 Senate split. Eight also fills exactly one row at
  this page width and gives one figure per beat - courts, markets, quake,
  economy, sport, politics - so the strip works as a scannable index of the
  whole day before the stories begin.
- **Ranking into hero / secondary / tertiary / briefs.** Stories were ranked by
  "would this lead a bulletin?": the Shenzhen three-way summit, the ICJ's
  Myanmar ruling and the Anthropic IPO each carry a different axis (politics,
  courts, markets), so they take the three hero cards. The next five stories
  still warrant a headline and a deck, so they form the secondary row; the
  seven after them are one-liners and form the tertiary row. Everything else -
  twenty-four items - becomes region-tagged evening briefs, whose job is
  breadth rather than depth: every continent appears at least once.
- **Continuous numbering, 1 to 38.** Cards and briefs share a single sequence
  so the "38 stories" claim in the masthead can be verified by counting, and
  the numbers double as a rank: lower numbers get bigger cards.
- **The date-check footer.** The footer exists because the poster makes a
  falsifiable claim: everything on this page happened or was first reported on
  Thursday 19 November 2026. It proves the calendar math (19 November 2026 is a
  Thursday), shows the editing actually happened by listing what was killed as
  not-Thursday - Myanmar fighting, the Gaza push, the 144-drone Kyiv attack,
  all Wednesday - attributes every wire, and restates the one-page, one-day,
  38-story count. It turns the poster's premise into something a reader can
  check instead of take on faith.
